# 0026. Devices with platform interrupts, roles and the store volume

Status: superseded by 0028

## Context

Chapter 15 has `devices` hold every device of the machine and bind each to a driver instance of its own, which reaches only its device and whose trap cuts the device off before its memory is freed. NaN v0.7.0 gives a bare kernel's first task its PCI functions and ports through `boot::Kernel`. A function is linear and a resource of the domain run holding it: its windows, interrupts, power and reset derive from it without a trusted premise, and once a keeper is given to it, its release or its holder's settlement hands it back to that keeper cut off. A platform device's line installs through `hw::IrqShared.install_platform` with its global system interrupt, trigger and polarity, which the MADT's interrupt source overrides change for an ISA line. A component's Env may be linear, a handler may postpone a request and answer it from a later handler through its runner capability, and a server answers the calls of any protocol whose operations its own schema serves. NaN gives a register access the class `irreversible(emit, actuate, erase, spend)` and cannot yet seal a lower one (N15). An ended keyed task keeps its node until its key is superseded, and each spawn walks the nodes kept (nan-language/nan#119). A value moved into storage another domain owns passes every chunk it occupies to that domain, still charged to the sender's cell when the two cells draw from different supplies. The store, which holds `/sys/instances`, `/sys/grants`, `/sys/config` and `/live`, comes in later tasks.

## Decision

- **Packages.** `devices` holds the device protocols, the `Fat` protocol of `fat`, bus paths, the `drivers` registry and its matching, partition tables, the AML evaluator, the volume view and the `devices` component; `drivers` holds one module per driver family, each contributing its `drivers` entry; `fat` is the `fat` component, serving the calls of `devices::Fat`.
- **The core keeper.** The kernel's first task gives every PCI function a keeper and holds it with its keeper, every port, and copies of the ACPI tables `devices` reads. The device root is the kind `devices::Root`, whose orders the keeper serves: the devices and their identifiers, a table, the platform devices `devices` declares from the AML namespace with their resources, the cut-off, reset and power of a device, the topology's changes, and the device events, which it writes to the core rings. Only the core's root may claim a device. A function its keeper reclaims is reset before its next driver claims it; one that does not answer the reset stays with the keeper, `failed(device.reset-failed)`.
- **Driver instances.** `devices` creates each driver and volume view through its `Control`: the runner adds the instance with its grants, a `Device` grant minting an `os::Device` claim on the bus path. The kernel serves a driver by claiming its device through the root and building the driver's linear Env from the function or platform resources; the Env's abandonment releases them, and a trapped driver's settlement hands its function back, cut off, for the next claim to reclaim. `devices` follows each running driver's lifecycle through `Control.changed`: an abandonment cuts the device off, a driver past its restart limit leaves it `failed(device.driver-failed)`, and a driver that does not start is cut off and retired. A driver's requests pending when its domain ends fail with `device.reset`, or with `device.unplugged` once `devices` has deleted it through `Control.unplug`.
- **Requests.** Each driver serves a protocol of its own that adds private operations, such as completions, to its device protocol's, and answers calls made under the device protocol. A request its device completes later is postponed and answered from the completion's handler. Each operation that reaches a register declares the class NaN gives a register access. `Block`'s `discard` is `discard_blocks`, `discard` being a NaN keyword.
- **Interrupts and DMA.** PCI drivers take MSI-X, else MSI, interrupts on CPU 0 through their function; a UART takes the line its `_CRS` describes, an ISA line as the MADT's overrides route it. An interrupt fn quiets the device and posts a signal that a keyed task turns into a message. The RTC is read on request and takes no interrupt. Functions are attached to physical memory and their buffers drawn in the physical space: DMA is unconfined until isolation comes.
- **Roles.** The class roles are resolved again whenever a driver of the class starts or leaves: `console` goes to the UART on the port the SPCR names, else to the first in bus path order, `entropy` to every entropy device and `rtc` to the first. `store-volume` goes to a store partition, on the boot volume's device first, else to the view `devices.store-img` over `/nanos/store.img`, which `fat` allocates from the boot volume's free space once it runs. `settled` answers once a try at placing `store-volume` has ended; a try made while `fat` does not run is made again for later requests.
- **Reused keys.** Keyed tasks run under keys that get reused, so the nodes NaN keeps stay as many as the tasks that can run at once: a view forwards each request from a slot its finished forwarding tasks free, and `devices` records its events in order from one task.
- **`fat`'s calls.** `fat` writes each FAT block once per run of entries it sets, and draws its calls to the volume through a cell of their own, so no chunk a call passes out of the instance holds the cache's blocks.
- **Before the store.** Driver and view specifications and their grants live in the runner, roles in its directory, which resolves a wire naming a role; `/live/devices` records live in the state of `devices`, and configuration is the defaults. `supervisor` starts each step after `devices` or `fat` once `devices` has settled the role holders the start order needs.
- **The log UART.** Until `journal` runs, the core writes its event log to the 16550 at port `0x3F8`, so `devices` binds no driver to it and the `console` role resolves among the other UARTs.

Alternatives rejected:

- **`devices` holding the functions:** a driver's function would have to leave the domain of `devices` for the driver's, and nothing would hand it back when the driver traps.
- **Drivers serving the device protocols themselves:** a component's messages are its protocol's operations, so a completion would have to be a public operation every client could call.
- **Mapping the platform lines through the I/O APIC directly:** NaN's runtime owns its pins.
- **`Fat` in the `fat` package:** `devices` would depend on `fat`, which depends on `devices` for `Block`.
- **A key per request or event:** each would keep its node for good and slow every later spawn, until the core's boot heap is exhausted.
- **`fat`'s calls through the cell of its state:** each call would pass out the chunk the cache's latest block lies in, charged to `fat` while that block stays cached, so allocating a large file exhausts its cell.

## Consequences

- The device protocols' declared classes are higher than chapter 15's until NaN seals lower ones.
- Device grants are trust escapes and DMA reaches all memory until isolation gives each group an I/O domain.
- Bindings, specifications and `/live/devices` records do not outlive a boot until the store holds them.
- A component whose messages leave it from the cell its long-lived state lies in strands a chunk with each message until that state is freed.
